Comparing a few different life cycles side by side highlights something easy to miss when studying just one: not every animal grows the same way, and the differences are genuinely interesting.

1. What Metamorphosis Is

Metamorphosis is a dramatic change in body form during an animal's life cycle — a caterpillar transforming into a butterfly is the classic example, where the young and adult forms look almost completely different from each other. Not all animals go through this; it's specific to certain groups, like insects and amphibians.

2. The Butterfly Life Cycle

StageWhat Happens
EggA tiny egg laid on a leaf
Larva (caterpillar)Hatches and eats constantly, growing rapidly
Pupa (chrysalis)Forms a protective case where the body completely reorganizes
Adult (butterfly)Emerges with wings, ready to fly and reproduce

3. The Frog Life Cycle

StageWhat Happens
EggLaid in water, often in a cluster
TadpoleHatches with gills and a tail, living entirely in water
FrogletGrows legs and lungs while the tail shrinks
Adult (frog)Fully developed, able to live on land and in water

4. The Chicken Life Cycle (No Metamorphosis)

StageWhat Happens
EggIncubated for about 21 days
ChickHatches already looking like a small, fuzzy version of an adult
Adult chickenGrows larger and develops feathers, but doesn't undergo a dramatic body-form change

Comparing this to the butterfly and frog highlights the difference clearly: a chick already resembles a chicken, while a caterpillar looks nothing like a butterfly.

✅ Ask "Does This Animal Change Shape?"

After learning several life cycles, asking a child to sort animals into "changes shape dramatically" versus "grows gradually" groups is a genuinely useful way to check real understanding, not just memorized stage names.

5. Observing a Life Cycle in Real Time

A butterfly-raising kit (caterpillars that arrive by mail and are observed through each stage) or hatching chicken eggs with an incubator are popular hands-on options, though even watching tadpoles in a local pond over several weeks works well and costs nothing. Real-time observation, even briefly, tends to stick far better than diagrams alone — pair this with our nature journaling guide to track the stages as they happen.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

What is metamorphosis?

A dramatic change in body form during an animal's life cycle, like a caterpillar transforming into a butterfly.

Do all animals go through metamorphosis?

No — many animals, including humans and chickens, grow gradually without a dramatic body-form change.

What's a good way to observe a life cycle in real time?

A butterfly-raising kit, hatching chicken eggs with an incubator, or watching tadpoles in a local pond over several weeks.
