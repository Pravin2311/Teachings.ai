Simple machines are everywhere once you start looking — scissors, doorknobs, ramps, and screws are all doing real physics work, just without any batteries or motors involved.

1. What Makes a Machine "Simple"

A simple machine has few or no moving parts and makes a task easier by changing the amount or direction of force needed. It doesn't add energy — it just makes applying the energy we already have more effective, whether that's lifting something heavier or changing which direction a push or pull needs to go.

2. The Six Simple Machines

MachineWhat It DoesHousehold Example
LeverA rigid bar that pivots to lift or move something with less effortScissors, a see-saw, a bottle opener
Wheel and axleA wheel attached to a rod that turns together, reducing frictionA doorknob, a rolling pin, a bicycle wheel
PulleyA wheel with a rope or cable that changes the direction of forceA flagpole, window blinds
Inclined planeA flat, angled surface that makes lifting easier by spreading it over a distanceA ramp, a slide, stairs
WedgeA pair of inclined planes that push or split things apartA doorstop, a knife, an axe
ScrewAn inclined plane wrapped around a rodA bottle cap, a jar lid, a screw

3. Hands-On Demonstrations

  • Lever: Use a ruler balanced on a small block to lift a lightweight object, moving the balance point to feel how it changes the effort needed
  • Pulley: Loop string over a pencil held horizontally between two chairs to lift a small object, comparing effort with and without the pulley setup
  • Inclined plane: Compare lifting a toy car straight up versus rolling it up a ramp made from a book

✅ Let Them Feel the Difference

The real "aha" moment comes from physically comparing effort with and without the simple machine — lifting something straight up versus rolling it up a ramp makes the concept concrete in a way explanation alone can't.

4. A Household Scavenger Hunt

Send kids on a hunt to find one example of each simple machine around the house — scissors (levers), doorknobs (wheel and axle), a ramp or wheelchair ramp (inclined plane), and a bottle cap thread (screw) are all common, easy-to-spot examples in most homes. This turns the abstract list into a concrete recognition skill.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

What makes something a "simple machine"?

Few or no moving parts, and it makes a task easier by changing the force needed, not by adding energy.

How many simple machines are there?

Six: lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, and screw. Many complex machines combine several of these.

Can kids find simple machines around the house?

Yes — scissors, doorknobs, ramps, and bottle caps are all common, easy-to-spot examples.
