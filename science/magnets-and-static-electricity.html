Magnetism and static electricity both involve invisible forces that are easy to demonstrate but genuinely interesting to understand — these experiments make both forces visible and explain the real science underneath.

1. What Makes Something Magnetic

Only certain metals are magnetic, mainly iron, nickel, and cobalt. Most other materials — including aluminum, wood, plastic, and glass — aren't attracted to magnets at all, which often surprises kids who assume "metal" automatically means magnetic (aluminum cans, for instance, aren't magnetic).

2. Magnet Experiments

ExperimentWhat Happens
What's magnetic? sorting huntTest various household objects with a magnet and sort them into magnetic and non-magnetic piles
Magnet through paperMove a paper clip on top of paper by dragging a magnet underneath, showing the force works through some materials
Magnet strength testCompare how many paper clips different magnets can hold in a chain, testing relative strength

✅ Predict Before Testing

Asking "do you think this will be magnetic?" before testing each object turns the sorting hunt into a genuine hypothesis-testing exercise, not just a demonstration — see our scientific method guide for more on this approach.

3. What Causes Static Electricity

Static electricity builds up when electrons transfer between two surfaces that rub together, leaving one surface with extra negative charge and the other with a positive charge. That imbalance is what causes the attraction (or the small shock felt afterward, when the charge suddenly equalizes).

4. Static Electricity Experiments

ExperimentWhat Happens
Balloon and hairRub a balloon on hair, then hold it near the hair to see it stand up and reach toward the balloon
Bending waterRub a balloon on fabric, then hold it near a thin, steady stream of water from a faucet to see the stream bend
Static-powered paper bitsRub a balloon or comb on hair, then hold it near small torn paper bits to see them jump up and stick

Static electricity is a temporary charge buildup that discharges quickly, genuinely different from the continuous, controlled flow of electrons that powers devices — both involve electrons, but they behave very differently.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

What materials are magnetic?

Only certain metals — mainly iron, nickel, and cobalt. Most other materials, including aluminum, aren't magnetic at all.

What causes static electricity?

Electron transfer between two rubbed surfaces, leaving an imbalance of charge that causes attraction or a small shock.

Is static electricity the same as the electricity that powers devices?

No — static is a temporary charge buildup, while device electricity is a continuous, controlled flow through a circuit.
