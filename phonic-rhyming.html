🎵 Quick Facts: Rhyming & Listening

This game plays a word aloud and asks kids to pick the picture that rhymes with it — sharpening listening skills and an ear for matching sounds.

Fun Facts:

  • Two words rhyme when their ending sounds match, even if they're spelled differently — like "bear" and "chair."
  • Hearing rhymes is one of the strongest early predictors of later reading success.
  • Rhyming helps kids notice the smaller sounds inside words, a skill called phonemic awareness.
  • Making up silly rhymes together at home is a fun, no-materials-needed way to keep practicing.
  • Nursery rhymes and silly songs are a natural, low-pressure way for toddlers to soak up rhyming patterns.

New words to learn: rhyme, sound, listen, match

Parent Tip: After a round, ask your child to think of one more word that rhymes with the answer — it turns a quick game into an extra round of practice.
