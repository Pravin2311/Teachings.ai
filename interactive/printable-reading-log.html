Why Tracking Reading Helps

A visible, growing log gives a child concrete evidence of progress that's genuinely easy to overlook day to day — seeing ten filled rows is more motivating than a vague sense of "I've been reading a lot lately." The log itself becomes part of the reward, not just a record of one.

Minutes or Pages?

Minutes tends to work better for younger or slower readers, since it doesn't penalize careful, attentive reading over speed. Pages can work well for older, faster readers who might otherwise feel a time-based goal moves too slowly. Either column can simply be relabeled to fit — the format matters less than picking one and sticking with it.

✅ Keep Entries Quick

Make filling out the log part of the reading routine itself, right after finishing for the day. A title and a checkmark is enough — a log that takes real effort to update tends to get abandoned fastest.
