Why Specific Recognition Works Better

Naming the specific achievement — "for finishing the multiplication tables" rather than just "good job" — makes the certificate feel earned and gives a child something concrete to feel proud of, rather than a vague pat on the back. The blank line for the achievement is deliberately left open for exactly this reason.

How Often to Use One

Certificates work best reserved for genuine milestones rather than routine tasks. Giving them too frequently dilutes their impact; saving them for real effort or completed goals keeps them meaningful when they do appear.

✅ Works for Older Kids Too

A plain, well-worded certificate — especially one naming a specific, real achievement — can land well even with older kids and teens who'd reject anything that feels babyish.
