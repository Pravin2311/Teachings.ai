🌱 Deschooling: Transitioning from Traditional School

Updated November 2026 · 9 minute read

Pulling a child out of traditional school and jumping straight into a full homeschool curriculum the next Monday often backfires — a decompression period first tends to make the whole transition smoother.

1. What Deschooling Is

Deschooling is a deliberate decompression period after leaving traditional school, without formal lessons or a curriculum, that gives both children and parents time to shed school-based expectations before starting a homeschool routine. It's not a formal homeschool method itself — it's the bridge period before choosing one.

2. How Long It Typically Takes

Time in Traditional SchoolRough Deschooling Guideline
1 year~1 month
3 years~3 months
5+ yearsOften 4-6 months, sometimes longer

A common rule of thumb is roughly one month of deschooling for every year spent in traditional school, though this varies significantly by child and by how the previous schooling experience went — a difficult or stressful school experience may call for a longer reset period.

3. What Deschooling Actually Looks Like

✅ Not the Same as Doing Nothing

Not entirely doing nothing educational — child-led exploration, reading for pleasure, and everyday learning still happen, but without assigned lessons, grades, or a set schedule. The goal is resetting the association between learning and formal school structure, not pausing learning altogether.

4. Knowing When to Start Formal Curriculum

A child showing renewed curiosity, asking questions unprompted, or expressing interest in a specific topic are all signs the reset has worked and structure can be reintroduced gradually. See how to choose a homeschool curriculum once that point arrives.
