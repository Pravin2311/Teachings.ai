Homeschooling can feel isolating without a deliberate effort to build community — co-ops and support groups solve two different, related versions of that problem.

1. Co-op vs. Support Group

A co-op typically involves parents actively teaching or organizing shared classes for a group of homeschooled children, while a support group is more focused on parent connection, advice-sharing, and social meetups without a formal teaching structure. Many families end up using both for different needs — a co-op for structured group classes, a support group for parent connection and advice.

2. Finding an Existing Group

Local library homeschool programs, community Facebook or online groups, and homeschool convention or curriculum fair contacts are common starting points for finding an existing group in a given area.

3. Starting One If None Exists

✅ Start Small

Starting small — even two or three families meeting regularly for a shared subject or activity — is a realistic starting point, rather than waiting to organize a large, fully structured group from the outset. A small, consistent group often works better than an ambitious one that's hard to sustain.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a co-op and a support group?

A co-op involves parents actively teaching shared classes; a support group focuses on parent connection and social meetups.

How can a homeschooling parent find a co-op or support group nearby?

Local libraries, community online groups, and homeschool conventions are common starting points.

What does it take to start a co-op if none exists nearby?

Starting small with two or three families is a realistic starting point rather than organizing a large group immediately.
