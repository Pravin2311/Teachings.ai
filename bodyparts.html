Learn Body Parts

🧍 Parent Tip: Make Learning Active & Fun

Turn body part learning into a movement game! Say a body part and ask your child to touch it. Try “Touch your elbow!” or “Wiggle your toes!” This kinesthetic approach helps preschoolers remember names faster, builds body awareness, and supports early science and motor skills.

🎮 How to Play: Body Parts Scratch Card

  1. Scratch the card to uncover a body part.
  2. Listen as the game says its name.
  3. Touch that part of your own body, then tap Next for the next one.

What your child practices

Naming body parts is one of the first vocabulary sets young children learn, and it helps them tell grown-ups when something hurts. Pointing to each part on their own body turns the game into a movement activity, which helps the words stick.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Play "Simon says" with the words from the game: "Simon says touch your ears!"
  • Sing "Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes" together after a few cards.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What body parts does the game cover?

It covers the head, eyes, nose, mouth, ears, arms, hands, legs, feet and stomach, the words toddlers and preschoolers use most.

Is this suitable for toddlers?

Yes. Children as young as 2 enjoy scratching the card, and pointing to their own nose or ears is a simple way to join in.

Where can my child learn more about the body?

The learning pages on the human body and organs explain what each part does, with fun facts and activities.
