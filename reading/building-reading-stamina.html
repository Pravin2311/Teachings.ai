A child who can read a page comfortably but falls apart after five minutes isn't facing a skill problem — reading stamina is its own thing, and it builds the same gradual way physical endurance does.

1. What Reading Stamina Actually Is

Reading stamina is the ability to sustain focused, independent reading for an increasing length of time — a separate skill from decoding or comprehension, more like physical endurance than a reading-specific ability. A child can be a strong decoder and still have low reading stamina, and vice versa.

2. Growing It in Small Increments

WeekTarget Length
Week 15 minutes, comfortable and low-pressure
Week 2-37 minutes, once 5 feels easy
Week 4-510 minutes

Small increments, often just a minute or two at a time once the current length feels easy, build stamina more reliably than large jumps — pushing too far too fast tends to trigger fatigue and resistance rather than growth. There's no need to rush the timeline; consistency matters more than speed.

3. Using a Timer Without Making It the Point

✅ A Marker, Not a Competition

A timer can help make the goal concrete and give a clear stopping point, but it shouldn't become the focus — the aim is comfortable sustained attention, not beating a previous time. Turning it into a race or a daily record to beat can add pressure that works against the goal.

4. Recognizing Fatigue vs. Genuine Growth

Fidgeting, rereading the same line repeatedly, or asking to stop are all signs the current length has hit its limit for the day — better to end there than push through, since a bad experience at the current length can undo progress.
