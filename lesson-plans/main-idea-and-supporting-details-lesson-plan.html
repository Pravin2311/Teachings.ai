📝 Main Idea and Supporting Details Lesson Plan

🎯 ObjectiveStudents will identify the main idea of a short passage and list at least two supporting details from the text
📊 Grade LevelGrade 2–4
⏱️ Duration40 minutes
📚 SubjectLanguage Arts — Reading Comprehension

📦 Materials

Needed for This Lesson

  • A short, grade-appropriate nonfiction passage (5-8 sentences)
  • Printed or drawn "hamburger" graphic organizer (top bun = main idea, filling layers = details, bottom bun = conclusion)
  • Whiteboard or chart paper

🎬 Procedure

Warm-Up (5 minutes)

Show a picture of a hamburger and ask what holds it together (the buns) and what's inside (the filling). Explain that a paragraph works the same way: the main idea "holds" the paragraph together, and the details are what fills it in.

I Do: Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

Read the short passage aloud. Model finding the main idea by asking "what is this passage mostly about, in one sentence?" and write it on the top bun of a hamburger organizer drawn on the board. Then model finding two supporting details from the text that back up that main idea, writing them in the "filling" layers.

We Do: Guided Practice (15 minutes)

Read a second short passage together as a class. Ask students to suggest the main idea, discussing as a group until reaching agreement, then fill in supporting details together on a shared organizer. Ask "how do you know that's a detail and not the main idea?" to reinforce the distinction — see our reading comprehension questions guide for more prompts like this.

You Do: Independent Practice (7 minutes)

Students read a third short passage independently and complete their own hamburger organizer, identifying the main idea and at least two supporting details.

Wrap-Up (3 minutes)

A few volunteers share their main idea and details aloud, comparing answers as a class to check for reasonable agreement.

✅ Assessment

Check that the stated main idea reflects what the passage is mostly about (not a minor detail mistaken for the main point), and that the listed details are genuinely found in the text and support the stated main idea, rather than unrelated facts.

🎚️ Differentiation

Support: Provide the main idea already written on the organizer, and have students focus only on finding supporting details. Extension: Ask advanced students to write a one-sentence conclusion that restates the main idea in different words.
