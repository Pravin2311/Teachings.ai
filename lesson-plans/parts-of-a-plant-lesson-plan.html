🌱 Parts of a Plant Lesson Plan

🎯 ObjectiveStudents will identify the root, stem, leaf, and flower of a plant and describe the function of each part
📊 Grade LevelKindergarten–Grade 2
⏱️ Duration40 minutes
📚 SubjectScience — Life Science

📦 Materials

Needed for This Lesson

  • A real plant with visible roots (a potted plant works, or a whole carrot/radish with greens attached)
  • Blank plant diagram printouts
  • Crayons or colored pencils
  • Chart paper or whiteboard

🎬 Procedure

Warm-Up (5 minutes)

Show the real plant and ask: "What parts of this plant can you name?" List student answers on the board without correcting yet.

I Do: Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

Point to each part of the real plant and introduce its name and function: Roots (anchor the plant and absorb water and nutrients from soil), Stem (supports the plant and carries water up to the leaves), Leaves (use sunlight to make food for the plant), Flower (helps the plant reproduce by making seeds). Use simple, concrete language rather than technical terms like "photosynthesis."

We Do: Guided Practice (15 minutes)

As a class, label a large plant diagram together on the board, discussing each part's function again as it's added. Ask "why does the plant need this part?" for each one to reinforce function, not just naming.

You Do: Independent Practice (7 minutes)

Students label their own blank plant diagram and color each part, then circle the part they think is most important and be ready to explain why (there's no single correct answer here — the goal is reasoning).

Wrap-Up (3 minutes)

A few students share which part they circled and why, comparing different reasonable answers as a class.

✅ Assessment

Check that all four parts are correctly labeled on the independent diagram, and that when asked, students can state at least a simple function for each part (not necessarily word-for-word, but showing real understanding).

🎚️ Differentiation

Support: Provide a word bank with the four part names for labeling. Extension: Ask advanced students to research and add one additional plant part (like seeds or fruit) and its function.
