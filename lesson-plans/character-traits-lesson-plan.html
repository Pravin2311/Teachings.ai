🎭 Character Traits Lesson Plan

🎯 ObjectiveStudents will identify a character trait based on a character's actions and words, and cite text evidence to support it
📊 Grade LevelGrade 2–3
⏱️ Duration40 minutes
📚 SubjectLanguage Arts — Reading Comprehension

📦 Materials

Needed for This Lesson

  • A short story or book excerpt with a clear main character
  • A list of character trait words (brave, kind, stubborn, curious, honest, etc.)
  • Chart paper or whiteboard
  • Paper for independent work

🎬 Procedure

Warm-Up (5 minutes)

Ask: "How would you describe your best friend's personality in three words?" Let students share, then explain that authors show us a character's personality through what the character does and says, not just by stating it directly.

I Do: Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

Read a short passage aloud where a character does something that reveals a trait (for example, a character who shares their lunch with someone who forgot theirs). Model the thinking: "The character shared their lunch even though they were hungry too — that action shows me the character is generous." Write the trait and the supporting action on the board.

We Do: Guided Practice (15 minutes)

Read a second passage together. As a class, identify a trait the main character shows and find the specific action or dialogue in the text that supports it. Discuss why a trait like "generous" is better supported by evidence than a vaguer word like "nice." Connect this to skills from our main idea and supporting details lesson, which also asks students to point to specific text evidence.

You Do: Independent Practice (7 minutes)

Students read a third short passage independently, choose one character trait word that fits the main character, and write one sentence citing the specific action or words that support their choice.

Wrap-Up (3 minutes)

A few volunteers share their chosen trait and evidence, discussing as a class whether the evidence genuinely supports the trait chosen.

✅ Assessment

Check that the chosen trait is a reasonable fit for the character based on the text, and that the cited evidence is a specific action or line of dialogue from the passage, not a vague restatement of the trait itself.

🎚️ Differentiation

Support: Provide a word bank of character trait options to choose from. Extension: Ask advanced students to identify two different traits shown by the same character and explain how they might seem to conflict.
