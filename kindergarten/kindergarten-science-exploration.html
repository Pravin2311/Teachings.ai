Kindergarten science isn't about memorizing facts — it's about building the habits of noticing, describing, and comparing that scientific thinking is built on.

1. What Kindergarten Science Covers

TopicWhat It Typically Involves
The five sensesDescribing what things look, feel, sound, smell, or taste like
Living vs. nonlivingSorting objects by whether they grow, breathe, and need food
Weather and seasonsObserving and describing daily and seasonal weather patterns
Material propertiesComparing objects by texture, weight, and whether they float or sink

Most kindergarten science curricula focus on building observation skills through these concrete, everyday topics rather than abstract concepts — a five-year-old can reliably sort living from nonliving objects long before they could explain cellular biology.

2. Curriculum Guide vs. Activity Guide

A curriculum guide describes what a typical kindergarten classroom covers and in what general order, while an activity guide gives specific hands-on experiments to try. This guide focuses on the former — the content and skill expectations — while our kitchen science experiments and nature journaling guides give the specific hands-on activities that bring these topics to life.

3. Why Observation Comes Before Reading

✅ Noticing Is the Real Skill

At this age, most children can observe, describe, and compare far more precisely than they can read about a topic — observation-based science builds real scientific thinking skills (noticing, comparing, describing) without requiring reading fluency as a prerequisite. A child who can describe exactly how an ice cube changes over an hour is doing genuine science, regardless of reading level.

4. Supporting It at Home

Simple daily habits — naming the day's weather together, sorting a basket of toys into "living things it represents" and "not," or comparing how two different leaves feel — reinforce the same observation skills without needing a formal lesson plan.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

What topics does kindergarten science typically cover?

Five senses, living vs. nonliving, weather and seasons, and basic material properties — concrete topics, not abstract concepts.

How is kindergarten science curriculum different from hands-on activity guides?

Curriculum describes what's covered and in what order; activity guides give specific experiments. This guide is the former.

Why does kindergarten science emphasize observation over reading?

Children can observe and describe more precisely than they can read at this age — it builds real scientific thinking without needing reading fluency.
