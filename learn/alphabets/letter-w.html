How To Write Letter W

To write capital W, draw four slant lines. To write small w, draw two small curves.

Fun Facts About Letter W

  • W is called double u.
  • W is one of the longest letters to say.

Practice Letter W

Kids can trace W, color W pictures, and find W words in books.

The Letter W Sound

Letter W makes a "wuh" sound, made by rounding the lips and quickly opening them, almost like starting to whistle. It's a smooth, gliding sound rather than a sharp one.

Classroom Activity

Splash hands in a bowl of water while chanting "W is for water!" Then pretend to swim like whales around the room, calling out other W words along the way.

Homeschool Activity

Look out a window together and describe what you see while saying "W is for window." Afterward, trace the letter W on paper, counting its four connected slanted lines.

Tips for Parents & Teachers

Parents: W can be tricky to write since it has four connected strokes — let your child practice each "V" shape separately before joining them.
Teachers: W pairs naturally with water play, a favorite sensory activity that reinforces the letter through a hands-on, memorable experience.

Frequently Asked Questions

What sound does the letter W make?

Letter W makes a "wuh" sound, made by rounding the lips and quickly opening them, almost like starting to whistle — a smooth, gliding sound.

What are some easy words that start with W for preschoolers?

Easy W words for preschoolers include water, whale, window, watch, and wheel — everyday object and animal words are especially memorable for young learners.

How do I teach my child to write the letter W?

Practice four slanted lines forming two connected V shapes for capital W. Lowercase w is the same idea, just smaller and rounder.

Why is the letter W called "double U"?

The name "double U" comes from how the letter was historically written — as two U shapes joined together. Even though it looks more like two V shapes today, the name stuck.

What activities help kids remember the letter W?

Splashing in water while saying "W is for water," tracing W in sand or shaving cream, pretending to swim like a whale, or coloring pictures of W words while saying each one aloud.
