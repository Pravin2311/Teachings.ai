The English alphabet has 26 letters, from A to Z. Five of them, A, E, I, O and U, are vowels, and the other 21 are consonants (Y can act as both). Every letter has a capital (uppercase) form and a small (lowercase) form.

Vowels and Consonants

Vowels (A, E, I, O, U) are letters whose sounds are made with an open mouth, with nothing blocking the air. Every English word has at least one vowel sound. Consonants are the other 21 letters, whose sounds are made by partly blocking the air with your lips, teeth or tongue, like the b in ball or the s in sun. Y is a consonant in yes and a vowel in my.

How to Practice the Alphabet

  • Sing the alphabet song while pointing to each letter, so children connect the names to the shapes.
  • Focus on letter sounds as well as names: the sound helps with reading more than the name does.
  • Practice writing with the Alphabet Tracing and Lowercase Letter Tracing worksheets, or the tracing game.
  • Play the alphabet game and the letter sounds game for quick review.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many letters are in the alphabet?

The English alphabet has 26 letters, from A to Z.

How many vowels are in the alphabet?

There are five vowels: A, E, I, O and U. The letter Y sometimes works as a vowel too, as in my and happy.

What number letter is each letter in the alphabet?

A is 1st, E is 5th, J is 10th, O is 15th, T is 20th and Z is 26th. The table above lists the position of every letter.

What order should kids learn letters in?

There is no single right order. Many families start with the letters in their child's own name, then add letters that are easy to tell apart, such as s, a, t, p, i and n, which can quickly make simple words.

How long does it take to learn the alphabet?

Most children recognize all 26 letters by name between about ages 4 and 5. Learning every letter's sound usually takes longer, and that is normal.

What is the difference between uppercase and lowercase letters?

Uppercase (capital) letters are used at the start of sentences and names, like the A in Anna. Lowercase (small) letters are used for most other writing. Most text children read is lowercase, so learning lowercase letters early helps with reading.
