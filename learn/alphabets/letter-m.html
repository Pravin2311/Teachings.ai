The letter M helps children practice humming sounds in language.

How To Write Letter M

To write capital M, draw two slant lines and two straight lines. To write small m, draw two small humps.

Fun Facts About Letter M

  • M sounds like humming.
  • Many food words start with M.

Practice Letter M

Kids can trace, color and match M words with pictures.

The Letter M Sound

Letter M makes a gentle "mmm" sound, made by pressing the lips together and humming. It's one of the easiest sounds for a developing mouth to make, which is why "mama" is often one of the very first words babies say.

Classroom Activity

Pour small cups of milk for a snack while chanting "M is for milk!" Then pretend to climb a mountain by marching in place, calling out other M words the class can think of.

Homeschool Activity

Look at the moon together at night (or a picture during the day) while saying "M is for moon." Afterward, trace the letter M on paper, pointing out its two connected humps.

Tips for Parents & Teachers

Parents: M is a great confidence-builder since it's one of the physically easiest sounds to make — most children can already say it before they start formal letter learning.
Teachers: Pair M with a taste-test activity using milk or mango — hands-on food experiences make the letter stick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What sound does the letter M make?

Letter M makes a gentle "mmm" sound, made by pressing the lips together and humming — one of the easiest sounds for babies and toddlers to make.

What are some easy words that start with M for preschoolers?

Easy M words for preschoolers include monkey, moon, milk, mango, and mountain — food and animal words are especially memorable for young learners.

How do I teach my child to write the letter M?

Practice two vertical lines connected by two diagonal strokes that meet in the middle for capital M. Lowercase m is two small humps side by side.

Why do so many children say "M" words early, like mama?

The M sound only requires closing the lips and humming, with no tongue placement needed — one of the physically easiest sounds for a developing mouth to produce.

What activities help kids remember the letter M?

Pouring and tasting milk while saying "M is for milk," tracing M in sand or shaving cream, pretending to climb a mountain, or coloring pictures of M words while saying each one aloud.
