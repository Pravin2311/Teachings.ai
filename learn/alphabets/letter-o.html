The letter O is the fifteenth letter of the alphabet. Capital O looks like O and small o looks like o. The letter O makes a round “oh” sound. This page includes a free printable Letter O worksheet with tracing practice and activities kids can use at home or in the classroom.

The letter O helps children learn round vowel sounds clearly.

How To Write Letter O

To write capital O, draw a big circle. To write small o, draw a small circle.

Fun Facts About Letter O

  • O is a vowel.
  • O looks the same in uppercase and lowercase.

Practice Letter O

Kids can trace O, color O pictures, and find O words around them.

The Letter O Sound

Letter O makes two main sounds. The short o sound is heard in words like ox and onion — this is usually the first sound preschoolers learn. The long o sound, which says its own name, is heard in words like ocean and open.

Classroom Activity

Peel and share orange slices while chanting "O is for orange!" Then take turns hooting like owls, calling out other O words the class can think of.

Homeschool Activity

Look at pictures of the ocean together while saying "O is for ocean." Afterward, trace the letter O on paper — since it's just one big circle, it's an easy, satisfying letter for early writers.

Tips for Parents & Teachers

Parents: Letter O is a great confidence-builder for tracing since capital and lowercase look almost identical, just different sizes — a gentle introduction to that concept.
Teachers: Pair O with a "round things" scavenger hunt — balls, plates, and clocks are all everyday shapes that reinforce the letter's circular form.

Frequently Asked Questions

What sound does the letter O make?

Letter O makes two main sounds: the short o sound heard in ox and onion, and the long o sound heard in ocean and open. Preschoolers usually learn the short o sound first.

What are some easy words that start with O for preschoolers?

Easy O words for preschoolers include orange, owl, ocean, onion, and ox — food and animal words are especially memorable for young learners.

How do I teach my child to write the letter O?

Letter O is simple: one big circle for capital O, and one smaller circle for lowercase o — often one of the first letters children can write on their own.

Why does the letter O look the same in uppercase and lowercase?

O, along with letters like C, S, and Z, keeps roughly the same shape in both uppercase and lowercase, just at a different size — an easy, encouraging letter for kids just starting to notice the difference.

What activities help kids remember the letter O?

Peeling and tasting an orange while saying "O is for orange," tracing O in sand or shaving cream, hooting like an owl, or coloring pictures of O words while saying each one aloud.
