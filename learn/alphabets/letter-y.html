Letter Y: Sounds, Words and How to Write It

Y is the 25th letter of the English alphabet. It comes after X and before Z. Y is a consonant, and sometimes a vowel. The capital (uppercase) letter is Y and the small (lowercase) letter is y.

How to Write Capital Y and Small y

Follow the numbers: start each stroke at its numbered dot and move in the direction of the arrow. The blue top line, dashed middle line and red bottom line match the lines on handwriting paper.

Capital Y (3 strokes)

  1. Start at the top on the left. Draw a slanted line down to the middle.
  2. Start at the top on the right. Draw a slanted line down to meet it in the middle.
  3. Draw a straight line from the middle down to the bottom.

Small y (2 strokes)

  1. Start at the middle line on the left. Draw a short slanted line down to the right.
  2. Start at the middle line on the right. Draw a long slanted line down to the left, going below the bottom line.

Small y has a tail: its body sits between the middle line and the bottom line, and its tail drops below the bottom line, like g, p and y.

Common mix-up: Y can be mixed up with V (no tail) and lowercase y with g (a curved tail). Lowercase y has a long straight slanted tail.

What Sound Does Y Make?

  • y (consonant): at the start of a word. Examples: yak, yellow, yes, yo-yo.
  • long i (vowel): at the end of short words. Examples: my, fly, sky, cry.
  • long e (vowel): at the end of longer words. Examples: happy, baby, funny, puppy.

Interesting Facts About Y

  • Y is usually a consonant, but at the end of words like my and happy it works as a vowel.
  • When Y is a vowel, it sounds like long i in short words (fly) and long e in longer words (happy).
  • Y is the second-to-last letter of the alphabet.

Activities for Parents and Teachers

In the Classroom

Hand out balls of colorful yarn and let children wrap it around their fingers while chanting "Y is for yarn!" Then taste-test yogurt together, calling out other Y words the class can think of.

At Home

Look up a picture of a yak together while saying "Y is for yak" — it's an unusual, memorable animal for young kids. Afterward, trace the letter Y on paper, pointing out its two slanted lines meeting a straight one.

Tips

Parents: Once your child knows Y as a starting consonant, point out how it changes jobs at the end of words like "happy" and "baby" — a fun stretch for kids ready for a challenge.
Teachers: Y pairs well with a "surprising animals" theme — yaks are unfamiliar to most children and make the letter memorable.
