Square Shape for Kids: Sides, Corners and How to Draw It

A square is a flat shape with 4 straight sides that are all the same length and 4 square corners. It has 4 equal straight sides and 4 corners. Below you will find its properties, how to draw it, where to spot it in real life, and a quick quiz.

A bright square for little learners to explore!

Square at a Glance

Sides4 equal straight sides
Corners (vertices)4
Angles4 right angles (90 degrees each)
Lines of symmetry4
Flat or solidFlat (2D)

📐 What Makes a Square Special?

A square has 4 straight sides that are all exactly the same length, and 4 corners that are all right angles, like the corner of a book. Its opposite sides are parallel. If you fold a paper square corner to corner, you get two matching triangles, and if you fold it edge to edge, you get two matching rectangles.

🌟 Fun Facts about Square

  • All four sides of a square are exactly the same length.
  • A square has 4 right-angle corners, just like a rectangle.
  • Chess and checkers boards are made of many small squares.
  • If you cut a square in half diagonally, you get two triangles!

How to Draw a Square

  1. Draw a straight line across.
  2. Draw two lines straight down from each end, the same length as the first line.
  3. Join the bottoms with a line across, the same length again.

🔎 Where Can You Find a Square?

You can spot the square shape in everyday life, like a checkerboard tile, a window pane, and a picture frame. Try going on a shape hunt around your house or classroom — how many squares can you find today?

Common Mix-Ups

A square and a rectangle both have 4 square corners. In a square, all 4 sides are the same length; in most rectangles, two sides are longer.

Quick Quiz: The Square

Answer each question, then tap it to check.

How many sides does a square have?

Four, and they are all the same length.

Is a square also a rectangle?

Yes. It is a rectangle with four equal sides.

Which is a square: a chessboard space or a pizza?

A chessboard space. Most pizzas are circles.

🏫 Classroom Activity: Shape Hunt & Matching

Send children on a hunt for square objects — a napkin, a window pane, a picture frame — and gather the finds on a table. Then play a matching game: draw several shapes on cards and have kids sort out only the squares.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Fold a square piece of paper in half diagonally to reveal two triangles, then unfold and try again the other direction. It's a hands-on way to connect squares and triangles while practicing shape vocabulary.

🔬 STEM Connection

Squares tile perfectly with no gaps — try covering a table with square blocks or tiles and see how neatly they fit together, then try the same with circles to see the gaps that form.
