Heart Shape for Kids: Sides, Corners and How to Draw It

A heart is a shape with two rounded bumps at the top and a point at the bottom, often used as a symbol of love. It has only curved edges and 1 corner (the point at the bottom). Below you will find its properties, how to draw it, where to spot it in real life, and a quick quiz.

📐 What Makes a Heart Special?

A heart shape is made of two rounded bumps at the top that meet in the middle, and two curved sides that come down to a single point at the bottom. It is symmetrical: if you fold a paper heart down the middle, both halves match exactly. An easy way to cut one out is to fold paper in half and cut half a heart along the fold.

🌟 Fun Facts about Heart

  • The heart shape is a symbol people use all over the world to show love.
  • It does not actually look like a real human heart — it is a stylized symbol!
  • Heart shapes have two symmetrical curved halves.
  • People draw hearts on cards, balloons, and artwork to share kindness.

How to Draw a Heart

  1. Draw a curved line from the bottom point up and around to make the left bump, ending in the middle at the top.
  2. Draw a matching curve on the right side, from the middle back down to the bottom point.
  3. Or fold paper in half, draw half a heart along the fold and cut it out.

🔎 Where Can You Find a Heart?

You can spot the heart shape in everyday life, like Valentine cards, love notes, and candy boxes. Try going on a shape hunt around your house or classroom — how many hearts can you find today?

Common Mix-Ups

Real human hearts do not look like this shape. The heart shape is a symbol, not a picture of the organ.

🏫 Classroom Activity: Shape Hunt & Matching

Send children on a hunt for heart shapes — on cards, stickers, and gift wrap — and gather the finds on a table. Then play a matching game: draw several shapes on cards and have kids sort out only the hearts.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Fold a piece of paper in half and cut a curved line from the fold outward, then unfold it to reveal a perfectly symmetrical heart. It's a simple, satisfying way to connect symmetry with shape recognition.

🔬 STEM Connection

The heart shape is a great way to introduce symmetry — fold it down the middle and both sides match exactly. Ask your child to find other symmetrical shapes, like a square or a butterfly's wings.
