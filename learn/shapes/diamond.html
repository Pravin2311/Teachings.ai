Diamond Shape for Kids: Sides, Corners and How to Draw It

A diamond is a shape with 4 equal straight sides that is tilted, so it has two pointy corners and two wider corners; in math it is called a rhombus. It has 4 equal straight sides and 4 corners. Below you will find its properties, how to draw it, where to spot it in real life, and a quick quiz.

Diamond at a Glance

Sides4 equal straight sides
Corners (vertices)4
AnglesOpposite angles are equal: two narrow and two wide
Lines of symmetry2 (top to bottom and side to side)
Flat or solidFlat (2D)

📐 What Makes a Diamond Special?

In math, the diamond shape is called a rhombus. It has 4 straight sides that are all the same length, like a square, but it is tilted so it has two pointy corners and two wider corners. Its opposite sides are parallel, which means they never meet. If you turn a square on one of its corners, it looks like a diamond.

🌟 Fun Facts about Diamond

  • A diamond shape is also called a rhombus by mathematicians.
  • All four sides of a diamond shape are exactly equal in length.
  • Kites are often made in a diamond shape to help them fly steadily.
  • The diamond suit on playing cards is named after this shape.

How to Draw a Diamond

  1. Make a dot at the top, bottom, left and right, like the points of a compass.
  2. Join the dots with four straight lines: top to right, right to bottom, bottom to left and left to top.

🔎 Where Can You Find a Diamond?

You can spot the diamond shape in everyday life, like a kite, a baseball field, and playing cards. Try going on a shape hunt around your house or classroom — how many diamonds can you find today?

Common Mix-Ups

A square turned onto one corner looks like a diamond. A diamond is a square only if all four of its corners are square corners (right angles).

🏫 Classroom Activity: Shape Hunt & Matching

Send children on a hunt for diamond shapes — on playing cards, kites, and road signs — and gather the finds on a table. Then play a matching game: draw several shapes on cards and have kids sort out only the diamonds.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Cut out a paper square, then rotate it so a corner points up — talk about how it's the exact same shape, just turned. This hands-on rotation helps kids see that a diamond and a square share the same 4 equal sides.

🔬 STEM Connection

Diamonds are the classic kite shape because their pointed top and bottom help air flow smoothly around them. Ask your child why a diamond might catch the wind better than a flat square.
