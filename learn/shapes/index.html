Why Learn Shapes?

Shapes are the building blocks of everything we see — from the wheels on a car to the windows in a house. Learning shapes helps children recognize patterns, build early math skills, and describe the world around them. Click on any shape above to explore its sides, corners, and fun real-life examples!

2D and 3D Shapes

All the shapes on this page are 2D (flat) shapes: you can draw them on paper. 3D shapes, like a ball (sphere), a box (cube) or a can (cylinder), are solid. Many 3D objects have 2D shapes on their faces: each face of a cube is a square.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the basic shapes kids learn first?

Most children start with the circle, square, triangle and rectangle, then move on to shapes like the oval, star, heart and diamond, and later the pentagon, hexagon and octagon.

What is the difference between a square and a rectangle?

Both have 4 straight sides and 4 square corners. A square's sides are all the same length, while a rectangle has two longer sides and two shorter sides. So every square is a special kind of rectangle.

What is the difference between 2D and 3D shapes?

2D shapes, like a circle or square, are flat. 3D shapes, like a ball (sphere) or box (cube), are solid and take up space.

How can I help my child learn shapes?

Go on shape hunts around the house, count the sides and corners together, and draw shapes step by step. Naming the sides and corners helps more than memorizing pictures.
