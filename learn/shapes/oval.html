Oval Shape for Kids: Sides, Corners and How to Draw It

An oval is a round shape that is stretched out, like an egg or a running track. It has 0 straight sides (just one curved edge) and 0 corners. Below you will find its properties, how to draw it, where to spot it in real life, and a quick quiz.

📐 What Makes an Oval Special?

An oval is a round shape that is stretched out, like an egg or a running track. It has no straight sides and no corners. A special, perfectly even oval is called an ellipse. Ellipses are important in space: the planets travel around the Sun in paths shaped like slightly squashed circles, which are ellipses.

🌟 Fun Facts about Oval

  • An oval looks like a circle that has been gently squished on two sides.
  • Eggs are oval shaped, which helps them roll in a circle instead of straight off a nest!
  • Ovals have no straight sides or sharp corners.
  • Racetracks are often built in a long oval shape.

How to Draw an Oval

  1. Draw a circle that is stretched out sideways or up and down.
  2. Keep the curve smooth all the way around, with no flat parts or corners.

🔎 Where Can You Find an Oval?

You can spot the oval shape in everyday life, like an egg, a rugby ball, and a mirror. Try going on a shape hunt around your house or classroom — how many ovals can you find today?

Common Mix-Ups

An oval and a circle both have no corners. A circle is equally round everywhere, but an oval is longer in one direction.

Quick Quiz: The Oval

Answer each question, then tap it to check.

How many corners does an oval have?

None.

Which is shaped like an oval: an egg or a box?

An egg.

What is the difference between an oval and a circle?

An oval is stretched longer in one direction; a circle is the same width all the way around.

🏫 Classroom Activity: Shape Hunt & Matching

Send children on a hunt for oval shapes — an egg, a mirror, a picture frame — and gather the finds on a table. Then play a matching game: draw several shapes on cards and have kids sort out only the ovals.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Set a real egg next to a round ball and ask your child to describe how they're different. It's a simple, everyday way to compare an oval and a circle side by side.

🔬 STEM Connection

An egg's oval shape helps it roll in a curve instead of a straight line, so it's less likely to roll off a nest — ask your child to roll a ball versus a toy egg and compare the paths.
