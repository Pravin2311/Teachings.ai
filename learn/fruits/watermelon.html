Watermelon for Kids: The Giant Juicy Summer Fruit

Watermelons are huge, green on the outside, and red and juicy on the inside!

The Watermelon is the ultimate summer fruit! It is huge, heavy, and wears a tough green striped jacket. When you cut it open, you find bright red (or sometimes yellow!) flesh that is incredibly juicy and sweet. As its name suggests, a watermelon is full of water—in fact, it is over 90% water! This makes it the perfect snack to cool you down on a hot day. Let's slice into some fun facts about this giant fruit.

💪 Why Are Watermelons Good for You?

Watermelons are a hydration superstar:

  • Hydration: Being 92% water, they help keep your body cool and hydrated better than plain water sometimes!
  • Lycopene: This is what makes the flesh red. It's a powerful antioxidant that protects your heart and skin from the sun.
  • Vitamin C: Helps your immune system fight off germs.
  • Vitamin A: Great for your eyes and vision.
  • Low Calories: You can eat a lot of it without feeling heavy because it's mostly water and natural sugar.

🌱 How Do Watermelons Grow?

Even though watermelons are huge, they don't grow on trees! They grow on long, trailing vines that spread out across the ground.

Farmers plant seeds in warm soil. The vines grow long and leafy, and yellow flowers bloom. Bees pollinate the flowers, and then tiny green watermelons start to form. Over summer, they swell up to become huge! Because they are so heavy, they rest directly on the soil. It takes about 3 to 4 months for a watermelon to grow from a tiny flower to a giant fruit ready to harvest.

Did you know? The heaviest watermelon ever grown weighed over 350 pounds (159 kg)! That's as heavy as a grown-up bear!

🌱 Seedless vs. Seeded Watermelons

You might see two types of watermelons in the store:

  • Seeded: These have hard black seeds inside. You have to spit them out or remove them while eating. These are the traditional type.
  • Seedless: These have tiny, soft white seeds that you can eat right along with the fruit! They aren't truly "seedless" (they start from seeds), but the seeds inside never grow hard.

Most people today prefer seedless watermelons because they are easier to eat!

🤓 Amazing Watermelon Facts

  • Family: Watermelons are related to cucumbers, pumpkins, and squash!
  • History: People have been eating watermelons for over 5,000 years. Ancient Egyptians even put them in tombs for kings to eat in the afterlife!
  • Yellow Inside: Not all watermelons are red inside. Some have yellow or orange flesh, which tastes just as sweet!
  • Thump Test: To pick a good watermelon, knock on it. If it sounds hollow and deep (like a drum), it's ripe. If it sounds flat, it's not ready.
  • State Vegetable? In Oklahoma, the watermelon is actually the official state vegetable because it's related to pumpkins!

🥣 Yummy Ways to Eat Watermelon

Watermelon is versatile and refreshing:

  • Slices: Cut into big triangles and eat off the rind.
  • Cubes: Cut into bite-sized chunks for a fruit salad.
  • Balls: Use a melon baller to make perfect spheres for parties.
  • Juice: Blend and strain for fresh watermelon juice.
  • Frozen: Freeze cubes for a cool summer treat that melts slowly.
  • Grilled: Yes! Grilling watermelon slices makes them sweeter and gives them a smoky flavor.

🧠 Quick Watermelon Quiz!

Question: What percentage of a watermelon is water?

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How much of a watermelon is water?

Over 90%, making it hydrating and refreshing.

How do watermelons grow?

On trailing vines across the ground, taking 3-4 months to ripen.

What is the difference between seeded and seedless watermelons?

Seeded have hard black seeds; seedless have tiny soft white seeds you can eat.

How do you pick a ripe watermelon?

Knock on it — a hollow, deep sound means it's ripe.

What activities help kids learn about watermelons?

The thump test, making melon balls, counting seeds, or coloring a printable worksheet.
