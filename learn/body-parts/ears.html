Ears for Kids

Your ears help you hear sounds — from your mom calling your name to your favorite song!

How Do Ears Work?

Sound waves enter your ears and travel to your brain. That’s how you hear music, voices, and even whispers!

Fun Facts About Ears

  • Elephants have the biggest ears in the animal kingdom!
  • Some people have attached earlobes, others have free-hanging ones.
  • Your ears never stop growing — even when you’re grown up!
  • Bats use their ears to “see” in the dark — it’s called echolocation.

More to Discover

  • Your ears help you balance, not just hear!
  • The smallest bones in your whole body are found inside your ear.

New words to learn: hearing, sound, balance, listen

Try This: Close your eyes and try to guess a sound just by listening carefully.

🏫 Classroom Activity

Have children close their eyes and guess everyday sounds (a bell, clapping, a book closing) just by listening. Talk about how ears also help us balance, not just hear.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Gently spin in a circle a few times, then stop and talk about how the ears help the body know it's off balance. Draw a picture of an ear and label the outer part you can see.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How do ears work?

Sound waves enter the ear and travel to the brain, which turns the signal into sounds we recognize.

Do ears do anything besides hearing?

Yes — ears also help with balance, using tiny structures deep inside.

Where are the smallest bones in the body?

The three smallest bones in the human body are found inside the ear.

Do ears keep growing?

Yes — ears (and noses) continue to grow slowly throughout life.

What activities help kids learn about ears?

Guessing sounds with eyes closed, talking about echolocation, spinning to feel balance, or coloring a printable worksheet.
