Hands for Kids

Your hands help you grab toys, hold crayons, wave hello, and give hugs!

What Can Your Hands Do?

You can clap, draw, build blocks, play piano, and even count to 10 using your fingers!

Fun Facts About Hands

  • Each hand has 5 fingers — 4 fingers and 1 thumb.
  • Your fingerprints are unique — no one else has the same pattern!
  • You use your hands to feel textures — smooth, rough, soft, bumpy.
  • Some animals have paws or claws instead of hands.

Let’s Learn More!

Trace your hand on paper and decorate it — add eyes, a smile, and arms!

More to Discover

  • Each hand has 27 bones, which is why your fingers can bend and move so easily.
  • Your fingerprints are completely unique — no one else has the same pattern.

New words to learn: fingers, grip, fingerprint, palm

Try This: Press your hand onto paper and look closely at your own fingerprint pattern.

🏫 Classroom Activity

Trace each child's hand on paper and decorate it with a face, then compare hand sizes across the class. Talk about how everyone's fingerprint pattern is one-of-a-kind.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Blindfold your child (or just close their eyes) and have them feel different textured objects, guessing what each one is by touch alone. Count to 10 together using fingers.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How many fingers does each hand have?

Each hand has 5 digits: 4 fingers and 1 thumb.

Why are fingerprints unique?

Fingerprint ridge patterns form before birth and are unique to each person.

How many bones are in each hand?

Each hand has 27 bones, letting fingers bend and move in many directions.

What can hands feel?

Hands feel textures, temperature, and pressure through nerve endings.

What activities help kids learn about hands?

Tracing a hand, studying a fingerprint, counting on fingers, or coloring a printable worksheet.
