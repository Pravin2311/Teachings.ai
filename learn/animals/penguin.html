Penguins for Kids

Penguins are birds that cannot fly, but they are amazing swimmers. They spend most of their lives in the ocean and come onto land or ice to lay eggs and raise their chicks.

Penguin Facts at a Glance

  • Animal group: bird (penguins have feathers and lay eggs)
  • Kinds: about 18 species
  • Home: the southern half of the world, from icy Antarctica to warm beaches
  • Food: fish, squid and krill
  • Biggest: emperor penguin, about 1.1 m (3½ feet) tall
  • Smallest: little penguin, about 30 cm (1 foot) tall
  • Babies: called chicks; a big group of penguins on land is called a colony

Where Do Penguins Live? (Penguin Habitat)

All wild penguins live in the Southern Hemisphere, the southern half of the world. Only a few kinds live in Antarctica. Most live along the coasts and islands of South America, southern Africa, Australia and New Zealand, and the Galápagos penguin lives near the equator. No wild penguins live at the North Pole.

A penguin's habitat has two parts. The ocean is where penguins find food, and they spend most of their lives swimming. The land or ice is where they rest, lay eggs and raise chicks. Some penguins nest on bare rock or ice, and some, like little penguins and Magellanic penguins, dig burrows or nest under bushes.

What Do Penguins Eat?

Penguins are carnivores: they eat other animals. Their food is fish, squid and krill, tiny shrimp-like animals that swim in huge groups. Adélie and chinstrap penguins eat mostly krill.

Penguins catch their food underwater and swallow it whole. They have no teeth, but their tongue and the inside of their mouth have stiff, backward-pointing spines that help them hold on to slippery fish. Penguins can drink seawater, because a special gland above their eyes removes the extra salt.

The Penguin Food Chain

A food chain shows who eats whom. In the cold Southern Ocean, a simple food chain with penguins looks like this:

🌿 Tiny ocean plants→🦐 Krill→🐧 Penguin→🦭 Leopard seal

The arrows point to the animal that does the eating. Penguins are both predators (they hunt krill and fish) and prey (other animals hunt them). In the sea, leopard seals and orcas (killer whales) eat penguins. On land, birds called skuas and giant petrels take penguin eggs and chicks.

Living things depend on each other. Many animals in the Southern Ocean, including penguins, fish, seals and whales, rely on krill. If there were much less krill, penguins would find less food, and the animals that eat penguins would be affected too. Scientists call this web of links a food web.

How Do Penguins Stay Warm?

  • Feathers: short, stiff feathers overlap like roof tiles and keep out wind and water.
  • Down: a soft, fluffy layer under the feathers traps warm air next to the skin.
  • Fat: a layer of fat (blubber) under the skin holds in body heat.
  • Huddling: emperor penguins crowd together in winter and take turns moving into the warm middle.
  • Clever feet: warm blood going to the feet passes close to cold blood coming back, so less heat is lost.

Amazing Penguin Facts

  • Penguins use their flippers to "fly" underwater. Gentoo penguins are thought to be the fastest swimming birds.
  • Emperor penguins are the deepest-diving birds. Scientists have recorded dives of more than 500 metres (about 1,700 feet).
  • Emperor penguins lay their eggs in the dark Antarctic winter. The father holds the egg on his feet, under a warm fold of skin, for about two months. He does not eat during this time while the mother is away feeding at sea.
  • Once a year, penguins grow a whole new set of feathers. While this happens they stay on land and cannot go fishing.
  • Penguins slide on their bellies over snow and ice. This is called tobogganing.
  • Some penguins are in danger. African penguins are now listed as critically endangered.

🏫 Classroom Activities

  • Penguin huddle (Pre-K to K): have children huddle close together, then let the children on the outside move to the middle. Ask: why would penguins take turns?
  • How tall is an emperor? (K to Grade 1): cut a strip of paper about 1.1 m long and stand it next to each child. Who is taller than an emperor penguin? Then try a little penguin, about 30 cm.
  • Food chain line-up (Grade 1 to 2): give four children cards that say tiny ocean plants, krill, penguin and leopard seal. Have the class put them in order and explain what each one eats.
  • Blubber glove (Grade 1 to 2): have children put one hand in a bag of solid vegetable shortening, then dip both hands in ice water. Which hand stays warmer? Check for food allergies first.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Pick two penguins from the table above, such as the emperor and the African penguin. Find their homes on a globe or map, then draw each one and add one fact you learned. Talk about how emperor penguin parents share the work of raising a chick, and what jobs family members share at home.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How many kinds of penguins are there?

Most scientists count 18 kinds (species) of penguins. Some count 17, because they disagree about whether the northern and southern rockhopper penguins are one kind or two.

Where do penguins live?

Penguins live in the southern half of the world. Only a few kinds live in Antarctica. Most live on coasts and islands near South America, southern Africa, Australia and New Zealand, and the Galápagos penguin lives near the equator.

What do penguins eat?

Penguins eat fish, squid and krill, which are small shrimp-like animals. They catch food underwater and swallow it whole, because penguins have no teeth.

What animals eat penguins?

In the sea, leopard seals, orcas (killer whales) and some sharks and sea lions eat penguins. On land, birds such as skuas and giant petrels eat penguin eggs and chicks.

Can penguins fly?

No. Penguins are birds, but their wings are stiff flippers. They use them to fly through the water instead of the air.

How do penguins stay warm?

Penguins have short, tightly packed feathers, soft down underneath and a layer of fat. Emperor penguins also huddle together in big groups and take turns standing in the warm middle.
