What are Electronics?

Electronics are devices that use electricity to work! They help us learn, play, talk to friends, and see the world. From the computer you use for school to the robot that might clean your floor, electronics are everywhere.

How Do They Work?

Most electronics have three main parts:

  • 🔋 Power: Like a battery or a plug, gives energy.
  • 🧠 Brain: A computer chip that tells the device what to do.
  • 🖥️ Output: A screen, speaker, or light that shows us the result.

Electricity and Electronics

Electricity is a kind of energy that flows through wires. Electronics are devices that use tiny parts, like computer chips, to control that electricity so it can do clever jobs, such as showing pictures, playing sound or doing math.

Staying Safe with Electronics

  • Never put fingers or objects into wall sockets, and keep electronics away from water.
  • Ask a grown-up before plugging in, charging or downloading anything.
  • Take regular screen breaks, and never share your name, address or school online.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between electricity and electronics?

Electricity is energy flowing through wires. Electronics are devices, like phones and computers, that use tiny parts such as chips to control electricity and do useful jobs.

How do kids learn how electronics work?

Start with simple devices, like a flashlight (battery, switch, bulb), then move on to more complex ones. Taking apart an old, unplugged gadget with an adult is a great hands-on lesson.

At what age can kids start coding?

Unplugged coding games can start around age 4 to 5, and simple screen-based coding tools often start around age 5 to 7.
