Why Learn Numbers?

Counting is one of the very first math skills every child learns. Numbers help us count toys, tell time, and understand the world in an organized way. Click on any number above to explore fun facts and practice counting! Recognizing a number by sight (like "7") and counting that high out loud are two separate skills, and both take practice. At home, look for numbers on clocks, mailboxes, and calendars to make counting part of everyday life. Zero is a special number too — it means "none," and understanding it is an important early milestone.

How Counting Skills Grow

  • Saying numbers in order (rote counting) usually comes first, often before a child can count objects accurately.
  • Counting objects means touching one object for each number, and knowing that the last number tells how many.
  • Recognizing numerals (knowing what "7" looks like) is a separate skill that needs its own practice.
  • Number sense means knowing that 5 is more than 3, or that 5 and 5 make 10, without counting every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I teach my child to count?

Count real objects together, touching one object for each number you say. The last number you say tells you how many there are. Short, everyday practice, like counting stairs or snacks, works better than long sessions.

Is zero a number?

Yes. Zero is a number that means none. It is also an even number.

Which numbers from 0 to 10 are odd and even?

The even numbers are 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. The odd numbers are 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9.

What comes after 10?

After 10 come 11, 12 and so on. 10 is the first two-digit number: it means 1 ten and 0 ones.
