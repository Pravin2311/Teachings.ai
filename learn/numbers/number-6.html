Number 6 (Six) for Kids: Counting, Writing and Fun Facts

6 is written in words as six. It means a group of 6 things, like six legs on every insect, like ants and bees. In counting, 6 comes after 5 (five) and before 7 (seven). 6 is an even number.

What 6 Looks Like

A ten frame has 10 boxes. Here, 6 of them are filled, so you can see that 6 needs 4 more to make 10.

Counting to 6

Count out loud and touch one object for each number you say: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The last number you say, 6, tells you how many there are altogether.

How to Write the Number 6

  1. Start at the top.
  2. Curve down to the left, all the way to the bottom.
  3. Curve up and around to make a small loop and close it.

The word is spelled S-I-X. Practice tracing 6 in the tracing game or on the Number Writing Practice worksheet.

Ways to Make 6

Two smaller numbers can be put together to make 6. These are called number bonds:

  • 6 + 0 = 6
  • 5 + 1 = 6
  • 4 + 2 = 6
  • 3 + 3 = 6

Try it with real objects: put 6 blocks in a row and split them into two groups in different ways.

Six in Real Life

  • Six legs on every insect, like ants and bees
  • Six sides on a honeycomb cell, which is a hexagon
  • Six faces on a die, one of a pair of dice

Fun Facts About 6

  • All insects have six legs. Spiders have eight, so spiders are not insects.
  • A hexagon is the shape with 6 sides.
  • Six is an even number: 3 + 3 = 6.

Quick Quiz: Number 6

Answer each question, then tap it to check.

What number comes right after 6?

7 (seven).

Is 6 odd or even?

Even. 6 can be split into two equal groups of 3.

If you have 6, how many more do you need to make 10?

4, because 6 + 4 = 10.

🏫 Classroom Activity

Open an egg carton (or a picture of one) and count each of the six sections together. Then look for a honeycomb picture and count its six sides, connecting the number to a real hexagon shape.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Build a tower of six blocks together, knocking it down and rebuilding while counting each time. Since six is past the "one hand" count, repeating it often helps it become automatic.
