1️⃣ Number 1: One

The number 1, written as "One"

The number 1 is written as "One." Numbers help us count everything around us — toys, snacks, friends, and more! Let's learn all about One.

🔢 Counting to One

When you count out loud, you say the number 1 as "One." Try counting objects around your room until you reach 1 — like counting one bright star in the sky!

🌟 Fun Facts about 1 (One)

  • One is the very first counting number.
  • You have one nose and one belly button!
  • Anything multiplied by one stays exactly the same.
  • One is the loneliest number — but also a great place to start counting!

✨ One in Everyday Life

You can spot the number 1 in many places, such as one bright star in the sky. Numbers are everywhere once you start looking for them!

✍️ Tracing & Writing Practice

To write 1, draw a single straight line from top to bottom — the simplest numeral to master. Our free worksheets library has printable number tracing and counting sheets you can print and reuse at home or in the classroom.

🏫 Classroom Activity

Give each child one object — one block, one crayon, one sticker — and go around the circle naming what everyone is holding: "I have one block!" It reinforces that "one" always means a single item, no matter what it is.

🏠 Homeschool Activity

Point out single things around the house together — one front door, one refrigerator, one you! Naming "one" objects throughout the day builds the concept naturally, without needing a dedicated activity every time.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How do I teach my child to count to 1?

Hold up a single object and say "one" clearly. Repeating this with different single objects throughout the day helps the word click.

How do I teach my child to write the number 1?

Number 1 is the simplest numeral: just one straight vertical line down — a great first number to trace.

What are good activities for teaching the number 1?

Point to one nose or one belly button, hold up one finger, or find single objects around the house — a single lamp, a single door.

Why is 1 a good number to start with?

One represents a single, whole item — the easiest quantity for young children to grasp before groups of two or more — and it's the simplest numeral to write.

What's a good age to start learning the number 1?

Most children can recognize and say "one" as early as age 2, often before they understand full counting sequences.
