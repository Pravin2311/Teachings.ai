✅ Decoding and Comprehension Are Separate Skills

A child can decode fluently and still struggle with comprehension, or vice versa — both need direct attention rather than assuming one automatically follows from the other. See Helping a Struggling Reader for identifying which one needs the focus right now.

Frequently Asked Questions

What order do language arts skills develop in?

Roughly: letter recognition, then phonics, then blending into words, then fluency, then comprehension and independent reading. Writing and grammar build alongside reading.

Is phonics the same as reading comprehension?

No — phonics is decoding letters into sounds and words. Comprehension is understanding what those words mean. Both need direct attention.

Where should I start if my child is struggling with reading?

See our guide on helping a struggling reader, which walks through identifying whether the struggle is decoding, fluency, or comprehension.
