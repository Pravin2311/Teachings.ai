Learn Birds

🐦 Parent Tip: Encourage Nature Exploration

Turn bird learning into real-world discovery! After playing, go on a "bird hunt" in your backyard or park. Ask your child: “Do you see any birds?” or “What sound did the robin make?” This builds observation skills, nature awareness, and early science vocabulary for preschool and kindergarten learners.

🎮 How to Play: Birds Scratch Card

  1. Scratch the grey card with your finger or mouse.
  2. When the bird appears, listen to its name and its call.
  3. Use Next and Previous to meet more birds, including the owl, parrot, peacock, flamingo and hummingbird.

What your child practices

Children learn to recognize common and colorful birds by sight and sound. Matching a call to a bird trains careful listening, a skill that also supports phonics, because hearing small differences between sounds helps with hearing the sounds inside words.

Tips for grown-ups

  • After playing, listen for real birds outside and try to describe their song: high or low, short or long.
  • Talk about what makes each bird special, like the owl's big eyes or the flamingo's long legs.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Which birds are in the game?

Birds include the sparrow, eagle, hen, owl, parrot, duck, flamingo, peacock, hummingbird and pigeon.

Are the bird calls real?

Each bird has a sound recording that plays after its name, so children can connect the picture with the call.

How can we use this outdoors?

Take the game's idea on a walk: stop, listen for a bird call, and try to spot the bird that made it.
