Learn Human Organs

🧠 Parent Tip:
Teach Body Awareness & Science Skills

Encourage your child to name the organ, describe its job (e.g., 'The brain helps us think'), and point to where it is in their body. Ask questions like “What does your stomach do?” or “Why do we need lungs?” to deepen understanding. This builds science vocabulary and body confidence!

🎮 How to Play: Human Organs Scratch Card

  1. Scratch the card to reveal an organ, such as the heart or lungs.
  2. Listen to its name.
  3. Point to where that organ is in your own body, then tap Next.

What your child practices

This game gives children their first look inside the body. Naming organs and pointing to where they are builds body awareness, and talking about what each organ does lays the groundwork for science and healthy habits.

Tips for grown-ups

  • Ask "What job does this organ do?" and check the answer on its learning page.
  • Feel your heartbeat after jumping, or take a deep breath and feel your lungs fill up.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Which organs are included?

It includes the brain, eyes, heart, intestines, kidneys, liver, lungs, lymph nodes, muscles, stomach and teeth.

Is this too advanced for young children?

Simple names like heart, brain and stomach are fine for preschoolers. Older children can learn what each organ does on the organ learning pages.

How can this support healthy habits?

Connecting organs to their jobs makes it easier to explain why we eat vegetables, drink water, brush our teeth and exercise.
