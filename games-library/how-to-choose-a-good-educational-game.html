How to Choose a Good Educational Game

Updated October 2026 · 9 minute read

📋 At a Glance

  • Biggest red flag: A reward that shows up regardless of whether the answer was right or wrong
  • Good sign: The core loop requires genuine thinking, not just tapping through to the next screen
  • Worth weighing: How many ad interruptions happen relative to actual learning content

"Educational" is one of the least regulated labels in the app and game world — nearly anything can claim it. A few concrete checks reveal whether a game actually teaches something, regardless of the marketing copy or where it comes from.

1. The "Educational" Label Problem

Because there's no formal standard behind the word "educational," it gets applied to everything from genuinely well-designed learning tools to games that are almost entirely entertainment with a thin academic veneer — a counting theme slapped onto what is, mechanically, the same game as a non-educational one. The label alone tells you very little; the actual mechanics tell you much more.

2. The Reward Test

One of the fastest ways to check a game's real educational value: does the reward depend on the answer being correct? If a wrong answer triggers roughly the same celebratory animation, sound, or points as a right one, the "game" layer has been bolted on top of content rather than built around it — the child can succeed at the game without actually engaging with the learning task underneath it.

✅ Watch One Full Round Before Deciding

Sit with a child through a few rounds and watch what happens after both a correct and an incorrect answer — the difference (or lack of one) usually tells you everything you need to know.

3. Other Signs of a Genuinely Educational Game

SignWhy It Matters
Immediate, specific feedbackTells the child exactly what was right or wrong, not just "try again"
Some form of increasing challengePrevents the game from becoming purely repetitive once a skill is mastered
The core loop requires thinkingSuccess depends on the actual skill, not on tapping speed or memorized screen locations
Minimal disconnect between theme and mechanicA counting game should actually require counting, not just decoration around an unrelated task

4. Red Flags to Watch For

🚫 Worth Watching For

  • Same reward for right and wrong answers — see the reward test above
  • Frequent ad interruptions mid-activity — these fragment attention and pull focus away from the learning task itself
  • A skill claim the mechanics don't actually require — a "reading" game where success doesn't depend on reading the words
  • No feedback at all — a child who can't tell whether an answer was right isn't building the skill, just clicking through content

5. A Quick Evaluation Checklist

  • Does a correct answer feel meaningfully different from an incorrect one?
  • Does success actually require the stated skill, not just speed or luck?
  • Is there some way the challenge grows as the child improves?
  • Do ads or interruptions happen rarely enough not to fragment the activity?

6. Frequently Asked Questions

What's the biggest red flag in a supposedly educational game?

A reward disconnected from the actual answer — if right and wrong answers get roughly the same celebration, the educational value is often thin.

Does a game need levels or difficulty progression to be educational?

Not strictly, but some form of increasing challenge helps prevent a game from becoming purely repetitive once mastered.

Are ads and in-app purchases a sign a game isn't educational?

Not necessarily, but frequent mid-activity ad interruptions fragment attention — worth weighing against the genuine content delivered.
