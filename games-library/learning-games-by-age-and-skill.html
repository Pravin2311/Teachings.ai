✅ Follow the Skill, Not Just the Age Label

A child who's solid on letter recognition but still building number sense will get more out of a counting game "below" their age than an alphabet game "at" their age — matching the actual skill gap beats matching the printed range.

6. How to Choose the Right Game

Start with whatever skill feels shakiest in everyday moments — hesitating on letter names, miscounting objects, mixing up shape names — and pick a game that targets exactly that skill. Once a game starts feeling too easy (fast, confident, no hesitation), that's the natural signal to move up to the next skill level rather than continuing to repeat something already mastered.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know which learning game is right for my child's age?

Match the game to the skill your child is currently working on rather than strictly the printed age range.

Should a child play the same learning game repeatedly?

Yes — repeated play builds automaticity. Once a game feels too easy, that's the cue to move to something more challenging.

Are matching and sorting games actually educational, or just entertainment?

Both, when designed well — matching builds visual discrimination and memory, sorting builds classification and early logic.
