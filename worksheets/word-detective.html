🕵️ Word Detective Worksheet

🎯 Learning ObjectivePractice blending individual letter sounds into simple three-letter (CVC) words — applying phonics knowledge to real word reading, not just isolated sounds
📊 DifficultyIntermediate — assumes solid letter-sound knowledge, this is the blending step that comes next
👶 Best ForAges 5–6, kindergarten
⏱️ Estimated Time10–15 minutes
🖊️ Materials NeededPrinted worksheet, pencil

👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

Model blending out loud before students work independently — stretch the sounds slowly ("mmm...aaa...ppp") rather than saying each sound as a separate clipped syllable, which is closer to how real blending happens. For students who guess at words instead of sounding them out fully, cover any picture clues and have them point to each letter while blending, to build the habit of decoding rather than guessing from context.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

If your child hesitates on a word, resist the urge to just say it for them — instead, prompt with "what sound does that first letter make?" and let them work through it. This worksheet is a natural next step after the letter-sound work in our phonics guide; if blending still feels shaky, that guide's troubleshooting section covers exactly this stage.

✅ What Successful Completion Looks Like

What to Check

Since exact words vary by printing, check for the process rather than memorizing a fixed answer list: your child should be sounding out each letter in order, then blending those sounds together smoothly into a real word, rather than guessing from the first letter alone or relying on a picture. If a word is read incorrectly but the sounding-out process was genuinely attempted letter by letter, that's still real progress — accuracy improves with repetition once the blending habit itself is solid.
