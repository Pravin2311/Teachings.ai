👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

Unlike our alphabet tracing and coloring worksheet, which pairs each letter with a small themed picture and traces directly inside the letter shape, this workbook gives each letter its own large artistic bubble form to color, followed by separate repetition boxes — five tries each for uppercase and lowercase. The bigger coloring space suits younger fine motor skills, while the separate boxes isolate letter formation from the coloring task.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

One or two letters a day keeps this manageable — the full A-Z set doesn't need to be finished in one sitting. Let coloring be unhurried and imperfect; the real skill-building happens in the tracing boxes below each bubble letter, where five repetitions build the muscle memory a single trace can't.

✅ What Successful Completion Looks Like

Not a Traditional "Answer Key"

There's no right or wrong coloring choice — success here is a completed, colored bubble letter and five reasonably consistent trace attempts per case, not perfect precision. Watch for a child rushing through the trace boxes just to get to the next coloring page; slowing down on even one or two of the five repetitions matters more than finishing all five quickly.
