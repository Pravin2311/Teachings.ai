👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

This worksheet has two parts that build different skills: object-counting pages ask students to count a picture group and circle the matching number from three close choices, while a separate section shows a pair of hands and asks students to recognize the quantity shown — a subitizing and finger-counting skill distinct from counting individual objects one by one. The later pages push totals into the teens and up to 20, a step up from our worksheets that stop at 10.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

If the finger-counting pages are tricky, have your child hold up their own fingers to match the picture before choosing an answer — physically matching the pose often makes the quantity click faster than counting fingers in an image. Save the higher pages (teens and 20) for once your child is solid on counting and number recognition up to 10.

✅ What Successful Completion Looks Like

Not a Traditional "Answer Key"

Each item has one correct numeral among the three circled choices — the picture count is fixed and doesn't vary by print. Watch for a child guessing based on which number "looks familiar" rather than actually counting; covering the answer choices while they count out loud first is a reliable way to check the counting itself is doing the work, not recognition of a common number.
