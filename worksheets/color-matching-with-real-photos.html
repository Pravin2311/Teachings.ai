👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

Illustrated worksheets are useful, but real photographs add something drawings can't: they help a child recognize a color across the natural variation found in real objects (not every "red" apple is the exact same shade). Point out that variation directly — "this apple is a darker red than that one, but they're both red" — since this kind of flexible color recognition is exactly what transfers to identifying colors in the real world outside the worksheet.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

After finishing the sheet, look for the same colors around your home or during a walk outside — this photo-based worksheet is a natural bridge to that kind of real-world color hunt, more so than a drawing-based sheet would be. See our colouring sheets worksheet for the drawing-based version of color practice if your child prefers that format.

✅ What Successful Completion Looks Like

Not a Traditional "Answer Key"

Since this uses real photographs, exact color groupings depend on the specific images in the print. Successful completion generally means: correctly grouping or identifying objects that share the same basic color family, even when the exact shades vary somewhat between photos — that flexibility is actually the point of using real images rather than a limitation to correct.
