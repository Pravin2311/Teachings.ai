👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

Mix a few known letters in with newer ones rather than drilling unfamiliar letters in isolation — this "spaced repetition" approach (reviewing what's already known alongside what's new) builds more durable memory than repeating only the hard cards. Keep sessions short and upbeat; flashcards work best as a quick, energetic routine, not a test. If a child hesitates on a card for more than a few seconds, tell them the answer and move on rather than letting the pause turn into pressure.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

A quick flashcard round during a car ride or right before dinner works better than a formal sit-down session — the goal is frequent, low-pressure exposure. Celebrate quick, confident answers, and simply say the letter name yourself for ones your child doesn't know yet, without dwelling on the miss. This pairs naturally with our letter recognition guide, which covers the bigger picture of how this skill develops.

✅ What Mastery Looks Like

How to Know a Letter Is "Learned"

Each flashcard has one correct letter name to check against. True mastery means your child names the letter instantly, without pausing to think it through — hesitation, even with an eventually-correct answer, usually means that letter still needs more practice. A useful routine: sort cards into a "knows it cold" pile and a "still learning" pile after each session, and weight future rounds toward the second pile without fully dropping the first.
