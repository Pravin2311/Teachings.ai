🍇 Fruits Vocabulary Matching Worksheet

👶 Best ForAges 5–7, kindergarten and grade 1

👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

Unlike our fruits coloring worksheet, which focuses on color association for younger, pre-reading students, this one requires actually reading the fruit name to make the match — a genuine early reading task using vocabulary students likely already know verbally. For students still building decoding skills, read each word aloud once as a group before starting independent work.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

This works well once your child has some sight word or phonics decoding underway — see our letter sounds worksheet if reading these fruit names still feels like a stretch. Since fruit names are common, familiar words, this is a gentler real-word reading practice than a completely unfamiliar vocabulary list would be.

✅ What Successful Completion Looks Like

Not a Traditional "Answer Key"

The correct pairing is simply each fruit name matched to its own picture — a fixed, straightforward answer that doesn't vary by print. Watch for a child guessing based on the picture alone without actually reading the word (a common shortcut) — covering the picture briefly and asking them to read the word first is a good way to check that the reading, not just the picture, is doing the work.
