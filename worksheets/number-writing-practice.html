👩‍🏫 Teacher Instructions

In the Classroom

Model the correct starting point and stroke direction for each number before students begin — most numbers have a conventional starting stroke that, once learned correctly, prevents habits that are harder to correct later. Introduce numbers in small batches rather than the full 0-9 range in one sitting, and circulate to check formation rather than just completion. Numbers that are frequently reversed (like 2, 3, 5) may need extra individual attention.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Instructions

At Home

Keep sessions short — a few numbers practiced well beats the whole sheet rushed through. Say the number out loud as your child writes it, and where possible connect it to a quantity (holding up three fingers while writing "3") to reinforce that the symbol represents an actual amount, not just a shape to copy. See our Math Hub for games that build number sense alongside this writing practice.

✅ What Successful Completion Looks Like

Not a Traditional "Answer Key"

Since this is a handwriting practice sheet rather than a question-and-answer worksheet, there's no single "correct answer" to check. Instead, look for: strokes that generally follow the guide, numbers that stay roughly within the line, and a consistent starting point across repetitions of the same number. Reversed numbers (writing 3 backwards, for instance) are common at this age and typically resolve with more practice, not correction pressure.
