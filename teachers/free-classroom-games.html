These games were built for young children to play on their own at home, but most of them work just as well with a whole class. Each table below lists what students actually do in the game, the age it was designed for, and the setting where it tends to fit best. The setting is a suggestion based on how the game works, not a rule — you know your class.

8. Before You Project a Game

  • Preview it on the classroom screen. Open the game on the device you'll project from, check the sound and see where ads appear on the page.
  • Turn on speakers. Most games say each name aloud. If a game plays background music, tap the speaker button in the corner to mute it.
  • Go full screen. Press F11 on most Windows laptops (or use the browser's full-screen option) so the game fills the board.
  • Test touch once. If students will play on an interactive board, try one drag-and-drop game first, because boards handle touch differently.
  • Keep it short. Most rounds take 5 to 10 minutes. One game as a warm-up or one center rotation usually works better than a long session.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Do students need accounts or a login?

No. Every game opens straight in the browser. There is nothing to sign up for and no form asks for a name or email.

Are the games really free? Are there ads?

Yes, they are free. The site is supported by advertising, and ads can appear on game pages, usually above the game or below it. Open the game on the screen you will project before class so you know what students will see. See the privacy policy for how the site handles data.

Will the games work on our interactive whiteboard, Chromebooks or iPads?

They run in a normal web browser and respond to a mouse or a finger, including the drag-and-drop games. Interactive whiteboards that act as a touch screen should work, but setups differ, so try one game on your board first.

Do the games need sound?

Most games say each word, letter or number aloud, and the listening games depend on it. Use speakers for whole-class play. Games with background music have a speaker button in the corner to turn it off.

How long does one game take?

Most rounds take 5 to 10 minutes. Many teachers use one game as a warm-up or a center rotation rather than a full lesson.
